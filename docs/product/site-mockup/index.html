The problem, as it shows up

Autonomy ends at the point of payment.

Agents plan, browse and call APIs on their own, right up to the moment something costs money.

01

Agents hit a paywall and stop

A per-use fee, an API that needs an account, a checkout. The workflow stalls until someone steps in.

02

Cards and API keys were not built for agents

Handing them to an agent means unbounded authority and no way to say what it may buy.

03

Nobody can tell which agent bought what

Finance sees a provider total, attributed to agents in a spreadsheet after the money is gone.

Accounting

Every payment appears in your bookkeeping tool.

Each purchase lands in your accounting tool as a draft supplier invoice with the evidence attached. Fortnox today, more connectors coming.

Why now

The rails for agent payments are being built right now.

Agents now have a machine payment protocol

x402 and Stripe's MPP give agents a native way to pay. The x402 Foundation launched under the Linux Foundation with Coinbase, Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Google, AWS and Cloudflare among its members.

The rails are scaling

Stablecoins settled roughly $390B in real-world payments in 2025. Agents already buy inference, data and gift cards over x402.

Settlement figure: McKinsey, 2025

The buy side is still open

Stripe, Coinbase and Cloudflare ship agent wallets locked to their own rails. The neutral control plane for the payer is the layer left to build.

Enforced on-chain

An over-budget payment reverts automatically.

Budgets are enforced on-chain at execution time, even if Haven itself were compromised or offline.

✓

Provably non-custodial

Funds stay in an account only you control. Haven never holds them.

✓

Nothing for an agent to leak

An agent's credential cannot move money on its own. Rotate it in one step.

✓

An exit that needs no Haven

Inspect and revoke every budget with only your wallet and a public RPC.

Mockup notes (not part of the page)

  • Every figure in the frames is fixture data taken from the app's own test screens (Research agent, 214.00 of 250.00 USDC, Ada Lovelace AB, invoice 1042). Nothing is presented as a customer's numbers.
  • Claims kept inside the CASP and copy guardrails: "appears in your bookkeeping tool" not "audit-ready"; Haven "relays" and "records", never "holds" or "transfers". No partner or customer names. Stripe MPP is no longer claimed as live.
  • Ecosystem names used in copy only where they are product facts already on the current page: Base, USDC, x402, Fortnox (the live connector). No logos. Say if Fortnox should go too.
  • "Talk to the founders" is a placeholder for the design-partner conversation; it can be a mailto or a form. "Built in Stockholm" is optional.
  • Header nav drops the MPP item. "Security" anchors to the enforcement band; a full security page exists in the docs.
  • Colour rhythm, top to bottom: navy hero, white, soft tint (how it works), navy (developers), white (accounting), soft tint (why now), navy (enforcement), white (comparison table), indigo (closing band), white footer. Dark bands never touch; the table sits white directly above the indigo close so its indigo header row leads into it. The header is transparent over the hero rather than sticky.
  • The marketing surface stays light-only, as decided in the dark-mode epic. Fonts: Inter Tight for headlines (a display cut of the app's Inter), Inter body, JetBrains Mono for commands and amounts.
