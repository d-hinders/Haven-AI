- **#1519** — The demo merchant stopped charging a buyer and then reporting the payment as failed. Once an EIP-3009 signature has verified off-chain, `transferWithAuthorization` has exactly two remaining on-chain failure modes — the authorization nonce already consumed, and the payer holding too little USDC — and viem raises BOTH as an unwrapped `ContractFunctionExecutionError` during gas estimation, which #1517 could only classify as a merchant-side FAULT. Neither is a merchant fault; both are facts about the payer, and the first one specifically means **the money already moved**. The merchant now reads `balanceOf` and EIP-3009's own `authorizationState` before submitting: a consumed nonce raises the new `AuthorizationAlreadyUsedError`, which `settleOnce` records as the SETTLEMENT IT DESCRIBES and serves the goods, and an underfunded payer gets a plain-language `PaymentError` naming the held and required amounts instead of a fault. **No authority, custody, or execution boundary moves.** This is a test fixture, not Haven product code: nothing changes about what may be spent, by whom, or under which caveats; the delegate key still never leaves the local signer; Haven's payment path, policy evaluation, funding leg and intent lifecycle are untouched, and the merchant gains no new ability to move funds — the two added calls are `view` functions. What changes is that the merchant can no longer take payment and answer HTTP 402. Recorded because the defect was a genuine money-safety inversion observed with real value on Base Sepolia on 2026-08-17: funding `0xc60f22a4…` moved 0.001 USDC Safe→delegate at 13:27:24, settlement `0x02e63937…` moved the same 0.001 USDC delegate→merchant at 13:27:28, both `Success`, the delegate left at zero — and the buyer was told `Payment failed`. Nothing was lost or stranded and no double-spend was possible (the second submission reverts at estimation and never becomes a transaction), but a payer told a completed payment failed is a payer who retries, and on a rail where retries move money that reporting error is the dangerous half. The chain is now the authority for "did this settle", which also makes the answer survive the process restarts that the in-memory `settled`/`attempts` maps do not ([#1515](https://github.com/d-hinders/Haven-AI/issues/1515)) — those remain the fast path, no longer the only one. The QA legs that surfaced this (`x402-settle`, `x402-delegation-3009`) were asserting on the merchant's HTTP status while the on-chain money flow was correct throughout; Haven's funding leg, delegation, allowance metering, signing and sweep all behaved correctly in every run examined. Mutation-proven: removing the already-settled branch fails the new test, which asserts a 200 carrying the goods rather than a 402. Perimeter unchanged.
