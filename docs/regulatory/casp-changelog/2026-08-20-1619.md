- **#1619** — the erc7710 direct-settlement lifecycle (`settleX402Erc7710` / `prepareX402Erc7710` / `submitX402Erc7710`) moved out of `HavenClient` into an internal `x402-erc7710.ts` module; the facade keeps the three public methods as thin, signature-identical wrappers. **Behaviour-preserving: no authority, custody or execution boundary moves, no wire contract changes, and nothing new is grantable.** Non-custody is untouched — `settle()` still signs in-process with the caller's own delegate key, and the `prepare`/`submit` split still exists precisely so the hosted topology can drive the two halves with the LOCAL signer in between, because hosted Haven has no key and must not. Neither half can widen what the delegation already permits; the caveat enforcers decide that on-chain, and the backend independently refuses erc7710 from a non-delegation account. Every refusal moved verbatim: the rail check before scheme selection (so a legacy-rail agent is told about its rail, not about the merchant), the never-silently-reroute refusal when no `accepts[]` entry carries `extra.assetTransferMethod: 'erc7710'`, the refusal to sign a payload whose returned `signature_scheme` is not what this path asked for, and the exact omission rules for `facilitatorAddresses` and `mcpCallContext`. The scheme's defining ABSENCE — no funding leg, so no delegate balance and no funding-transaction wait — is now pinned rather than assumed: a test drives all three public entry points against a real client with the funding leg's on-chain reads spied, and mutation-proving it required widening the test to the facade wrappers, since the module cannot reach a funding leg at all. Perimeter unchanged.
