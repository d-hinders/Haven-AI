- **#1987** — Slice 4 of the Safe-rail retirement (epic #1440) **deletes the AllowanceModule execution machinery**. #1984 closed the inflow, #1986 closed the spend with HTTP 410; this removes the code those refusals were standing in front of. Haven can no longer construct, sign-verify, relay or nonce-coordinate an AllowanceModule transfer, because the functions that did so are gone: `executeAllowanceTransfer`, `generateTransferHash` and `recoverSigner` (with the `executeAllowanceTransfer` ABI fragment and the relayer spend-guard / send-lock wiring they needed), the cross-replica allowance-nonce coordinator and its watermark repository, the legacy x402 orchestration `modules/x402/legacy-authorize.ts`, the legacy MPP orchestration `modules/mpp/authorize.ts`, the legacy execution bodies inside `routes/payments.ts` and `modules/mpp/send.ts`, the per-token policy gate `hasTokenAllowanceConfigured`, and the off-chain coverage arithmetic `domain/payment-coverage.ts` (`decideCoverage`). **The perimeter narrows and nothing is added:** no new authority, no new custody relationship, no new route, no new signing scheme, no migration, no table dropped (that is #1990), and no funds moved — the on-chain wind-down of the relayer-owned Safe remains #1985, an operator-run step, deliberately not automated.

  **What deliberately STAYS, each with its sharing reason, because a deletion slice is judged by what it kept as much as by what it removed.** (1) **Sweep** (`rails/sweep.ts`, `modules/mpp/sweep.ts`) and the **delegate-balance monitor** — funds *recovery*, not spending; closing them alongside spending would strand exactly the money this retirement exists to make safe, and the #834 owner decision keeps them while any funding-leg rail lives. (2) Everything the **#946 EIP-3009 bridge** shares with them, including `modules/mpp/evidence.ts`'s post-settlement delegate reconciliation. Both were verified by their existing suites passing **unmodified** against the deleted tree. (3) The **rail seam** `rails/execution-rail.ts` — byte-identical to `dev` in this diff, reversibility per #834, with both tombstones and the `not delegation && not session` predicate intact. (4) `getProvider`, `getRelayerWallet` and `getTokenBalance`, which live in the retired rail's file but are not AllowanceModule code at all — the first two are one-line delegations to `infra/relayer.ts` (#1533's single nonce view) and the third is a generic native/ERC-20 balance read; re-homing live shared infrastructure is a refactor, not a deletion, and does not belong in a money-path deletion. (5) The four **contract READS** — `getTokenAllowance`, `getLatestBlockTimeSec`, `computeEffectiveAllowance`, `getTokensForDelegate` — which back two surfaces #1986 deliberately left open: `GET /machine-payments/allowances` (the epic's "accounts and history stay READABLE" boundary) and the legacy wallet-approval authority check in `routes/agent-connection-setups.ts`. Both are reads; neither can spend.

  **The regulatory consequence that matters, reported rather than absorbed: this deletion silently hollowed out a Red Line #4 proof, and the fix is a stronger guard, not a smaller one.** #1986 proved "the delegation branch performs no off-chain coverage arithmetic" with `not.toHaveBeenCalled()` spies on `computeEffectiveAllowance` / `getTokenAllowance` / `generateTransferHash`. That was a genuine behavioural claim while `routes/payments.ts` still imported those functions and picked a branch at runtime. Once this slice deleted the legacy branch and the arithmetic, the spies became **true by construction** — a guard whose glob matches the empty set, which would pass just as happily if the entire payment route were deleted. Measured, not assumed: re-adding a banned import turned the new structural assertion red while every one of the old spies stayed green. The red line is therefore now asserted **structurally**, over the payment route's real import bindings parsed from source — the arithmetic is not merely unused, it is not reachable, because it is not bound into the module at all — and the assertion carries a **positive control proving the extractor can say yes** before any "no" is allowed to count. Blinding the extractor turns it red. The enforcer-level gap #1986 filed as **#2004** and wired into **#1991** is untouched and still open; this slice neither closes nor widens it.

  **Fail-closed was re-measured after the bodies were removed, because a 410 in front of nothing is not self-evidently still a 410.** Dropping the `POST /payments/:id/sign` intent gate turns **9** named assertions red; dropping the `POST /payments` account gate turns **6** red; and forking the seam so a DELEGATION account is called retired turns **4** red — all four of them positive controls, which is the result that proves the surviving suites still distinguish the live rail from the dead one rather than having collapsed into refusal-only guards. `routes/__tests__/allowance-rail-retired.test.ts` — #1986's tombstone suite — passes **22/22 unmodified** against the deleted tree, and a delegation-rail account still creates a signable intent in the same run.

  **One earlier entry in this log is superseded and says so here, because that log is frozen (#1496) and cannot be edited in place.** The **#1328** entry recorded that "the rail-agnostic `authorizeMachinePayment` orchestration in `modules/mpp/authorize.ts` (kept, unreferenced by any route today, for a possible future non-demo MPP rail)" was deliberately preserved. **It is deleted by this slice.** The reasoning that justified keeping it did not survive #1986: an orchestration with no route, whose one remaining rail is retired and 410'd, is not a seam held open for a future rail — it is an unreachable guard kept alive by its own tests, and 400 lines of replay machinery is a large thing to keep on that basis. The refusals #1328 actually relied on are untouched and still route-level: `POST /machine-payments/authorize` still answers its 410 compatibility tombstone from `routes/machine-payments.ts`, and the historical-read preservation it mutation-proved (mpp_demo rows stay readable) is unchanged. `covers:` also drops `packages/backend/src/domain/payment-coverage.ts`, which no longer resolves to a file.

  **`haven-reviewer` raised under-deletion and it was half right, which is the useful kind.** Five repository helpers lost their only caller when `modules/mpp/authorize.ts` went: `recordMachineIntentSignature`, `confirmMachineIntent`, `failMachineIntent`, `findMachineApprovalByKeyOrChallenge` and `findMachineIntentByKeyOrChallenge`. The first four are **deleted** — "the repository layer is shared" is not a reason to keep a function with no callers at all. The fifth is **kept**, and enumerating consumers rather than trusting the list is what separated it from the other four: it is the READ half of `insertMachineIntent`'s `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` replay contract, `insertMachineIntent` is LIVE on the delegation rail (`modules/x402/delegation-authorize.ts`), and the real-Postgres repository test that proves that contract (epic #1219) cannot assert "the replay returns null and the caller reloads the winner" without it. Deleting it would have meant weakening a live money-path test to make a deletion look tidier. The three `approval_requests` INSERT helpers (`insertPaymentApproval`, `insertSendApproval`, `insertMachineApproval`) are equally uncalled now and are handed to **#1990** as one unit: they are retired with the table itself, and `rails/execution-rail.ts` names all three by hand in the prose justifying #1986's approval 410, so removing one sibling would require editing the rail seam — which this diff keeps byte-identical to `dev` on purpose.
