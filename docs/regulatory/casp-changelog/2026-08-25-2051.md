- **#2051** — a **merchant-steerable bypass of a user-stated spending cap** on the
  already-shipped hosted tools `haven_pay_mcp_tool` (#1456) and
  `haven_prepare_catalog_purchase` (#1547), fixed here. This is a hotfix: the
  defect was live on `dev` and reachable by any caller.
- **What was wrong, stated precisely.** #1453 made `selectStandardPaymentOption`
  and `selectErc7710PaymentOption` mutually exclusive by construction. Both tools
  asserted the cap against the *standard* entry and then let `prepareX402Erc7710`
  independently re-select and authorize the *erc7710* entry, with no cap check of
  its own; nothing tied the two entries' amounts together. Because
  `payment_required` is **merchant-controlled input**, the merchant therefore chose
  which entry the user's cap was compared against. That is a control an attacker
  steers, not a guard that merely fails to bind. Measured on the shipped
  `haven_pay_mcp_tool`: a stated cap of 1 USDC against a merchant advertising
  `accepts[0] exact 1000000` (untagged) and `accepts[1] exact 900000000`
  (`extra.assetTransferMethod: 'erc7710'`) authorized **900 USDC** and returned
  `amount_atomic: 1000000`. `haven_prepare_catalog_purchase` was **unmeasured**
  when the issue was filed and is now **measured exploitable in exactly the same
  shape** — its merchant comes from a curated catalog row, but the 402 is still
  fetched live from that merchant, so curation constrains *who* can do this, not
  *whether* it works.
- **The receipt lied too, and that is the same root cause.** Both tools reported
  `quote.amountAtomic` — the price of the entry that was NOT selected. An agent
  that logs its own tool response saw `1 USDC` and had no way to detect what had
  happened. Fixing the cap without fixing the reported amount would have left a
  silent-misreport bug behind, so both are fixed together, in the top-level
  response fields **and** in `agent_summary`, which is what an agent surfaces to
  the user.
- **Blast radius, neither overstated nor understated.** There is **no backend
  backstop for the cap**: `runDelegationAuthorize`
  (`modules/x402/delegation-authorize.ts`) takes `amountRaw` as given and
  re-validates it against nothing, so the client is the only place `max_amount`
  exists at all. What still binds is the **budget**, on-chain, enforced by the
  caveat enforcer at merchant redemption — a different mechanism protecting a
  different thing. So exposure is bounded by the agent's remaining delegation
  budget rather than unbounded; within that budget the user's explicit cap was
  simply not a control. This is not a theoretical finding and it is not unbounded
  loss.
- **The fix, and why it is structural rather than a second special case.** The
  scheme is now selected **first**, and the cap is asserted exactly **once**,
  against `x402AuthorizationAmount(selection.option)`, converted with **that
  option's own asset and decimals** (a human cap of "1" means 1 of the asset being
  paid, never a different entry's). Keeping the old pre-selection check alongside a
  new one leaves the mirror-image defect: an expensive standard entry beside a
  cheap erc7710 entry **over-binds** and refuses a purchase citing an amount that
  would never have been authorized — fail-safe, but it makes stating a spending
  limit the thing that breaks a payable purchase, and #2052 proved that direction
  live (3 USDC standard / 0.50 USDC erc7710 under a 1 USDC cap returned
  `PRICE_EXCEEDS_MAX` for 3 USDC). Two cap checks guarding two mutually exclusive
  selectors is how this bug occurred in both directions; there is now one check.
- **One control, one function, three callers.** The check is extracted as
  `priceSelectedOption` in `packages/mcp-server/src/tools.ts` and called from all
  three hosted call sites — `haven_pay_x402_quote` (whose inline version #2052
  established and which is refactored onto the helper here with no behavioural
  change), `haven_pay_mcp_tool`, and `haven_prepare_catalog_purchase`. Three inline
  copies of one spending control is precisely how this got fixed in one place and
  left standing in two.
- **The same steer was aimed at the OTHER client-side guard, and is closed too.**
  `haven_prepare_catalog_purchase`'s delegation-budget pre-check compared
  `quote.amountAtomic` against the remaining budget and matched the allowance row by
  `quote.asset` — so a cheap standard entry sailed past a small remaining budget
  while an expensive erc7710 entry was what got authorized. It now reads the
  selected option's amount and asset. `CATALOG_PRICE_DIFFERS` and the
  `ALLOWANCE_READ_OPTIMISTIC` message likewise describe the amount actually being
  authorized.
- **Ordering, and what protects money.** The cap and budget checks moved below a
  read-only `GET /machine-payments/agent` (needed to know the rail before the
  scheme can be selected). On `haven_pay_mcp_tool` that prefetch cannot throw
  (`.then(a => a, () => undefined)`), so no error ordering changes at all. On
  `haven_prepare_catalog_purchase` the agent read was already a hard pre-intent
  refusal one step earlier, so the only change is that a failing agent read now
  surfaces ahead of a cap violation. **No intent of either kind — funding or
  settlement child — is created on either path before the cap is asserted**, which
  is the property that protects money, and it is pinned by tests rather than
  asserted here.
- **No new authority, no new custody, no new route, no wire change.** No enum, no
  endpoint, no scheme, and no signing payload is added or altered. Every change is
  a client-side pre-network refusal becoming correct, or a response field
  reporting a number that was already being sent. The backend's
  `validateGenericSchemeRail` / `deriveFundingShape` guards are untouched and
  remain the authority on which shape an account may use.
- **Known limitation, recorded rather than implied.** Both tools quote through
  `buildX402Quote`, which throws when `selectStandardPaymentOption` finds nothing —
  so a merchant advertising **only** an erc7710 entry is unreachable through these
  two tools regardless of any cap. That is pre-existing, orthogonal to this defect,
  and deliberately out of scope here; filed as a follow-up.
