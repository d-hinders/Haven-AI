- **#2082** — `POST /x402/authorize`'s **erc7710 direct-settlement branch** now
  refuses an over-budget amount at authorize instead of handing back a signable
  settlement child. **This NARROWS what Haven will construct and grants nothing.**
  The gate is, and remains, the budget delegation's on-chain caveat stack: the
  `ERC20PeriodTransferEnforcer` reverted an over-budget redemption before this
  change and reverts one after it, the child was and is chained under the
  parent budget, and no amount that the chain would have refused becomes payable
  because of this commit. What moved is *when* the refusal reaches the agent.
  Measured live against the shared dev backend on 2026-08-25 (#1993, epic
  #1440): with a live remaining budget of 1.000000 USDC, a 2.000000 USDC request
  returned **201 `pending_signature` with `sign_data`** on this branch, while
  `POST /payments` and the EIP-3009 funding shape both returned 502 with no
  intent row and a decoded `ERC20PeriodTransferEnforcer:transfer-amount-exceeded`.
  Funds were never at risk in the 201 case — the refusal simply arrived at
  merchant redemption, after a signature and four round trips — but since #1450
  made erc7710 the PREFERRED scheme, the path most payments take was the one
  that refused latest, and the #420 invariant ("refused before it becomes
  signable") was false on it. Owner decision recorded 2026-08-26: pre-check.
  **Mechanism:** one additional read of the SELECTED budget delegation's live
  remaining period budget, through the existing `readRemainingBudget` (#1145) —
  the same enforcer storage read `GET /machine-payments/allowances` already
  performs, no new infrastructure, no new authority, no new key material and no
  new outbound surface. Over budget → `403` `delegation_budget_exceeded` with
  `phase: insufficient_funds`, `next_action: fund_safe_or_raise_allowance` and
  the shortfall, emitted BEFORE the settlement child is built, before the intent
  row is written and before the relayer-paid delegate-account deploy (#1667) —
  so the refusal creates nothing to reconcile and costs no gas. **Fail-OPEN, by
  construction and pinned by test:** `fromChain: false` (a failed enforcer read,
  or a delegation carrying no period caveat this reader can speak for) never
  refuses, a thrown read never refuses, an UNPARSEABLE remaining value never
  refuses (the `BigInt` parse lives inside the same guard as the read, so a
  malformed number degrades instead of 500-ing a fundable payment), and the
  fallback passed to the reader is the requested amount so even a dropped
  `fromChain` guard could only compare `amount < amount`. Refusing on a degraded RPC read would convert a transient
  outage into a stopped agent — the posture of #1145's fallback and #1319's
  `remaining_is_from_chain` flag. The boundary case is explicit: `remaining ==
  amount` is ALLOWED (`<`, not `<=`), so the final payment of a period is not
  stranded behind a refusal the chain would not have made. **Untouched:** the
  EIP-3009 funding branch (it already refuses at authorize; a second pre-check
  would be a second source of truth for one condition), settlement, signing,
  custody, credential handling, and every existing refusal. Guards are
  mutation-proven — severing the pre-check fails 2 tests, flipping `<` to `<=`
  fails 1, dropping the `fromChain` fail-open guard fails 1, and moving the
  `BigInt` parse outside the guard fails 1. A new QA leg
  (`x402-erc7710-over-budget-rejected`) asserts the refusal live against dev and
  is built so a MISSING-delegation 403, a rail-retirement 410 and a control that
  came back on the 3009 scheme all fail it rather than pass it. Perimeter
  unchanged (`modules/x402/**` was already on it).
