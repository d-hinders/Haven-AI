- **#2213** — a settled erc7710 payment can no longer be recorded as *completed*
  when no `machine_payment_evidence` row was written, and such a payment is no
  longer stranded outside every retry path. **No authority moved, no spend limit
  changed, no signing scheme touched, and no fund movement was added, changed or
  initiated.** Both halves of this change are reporting and recovery around
  money that has already moved on-chain; Haven still custodies nothing, signs no
  settlement and broadcasts no transaction on this path.
  **The defect, against merged code.** PR #2135 shipped the #2117 passive
  settlement sweep. Its `sweepOne` called the evidence seam and could not
  observe the result: `tryRecordMachinePaymentEvidenceBaseById` returned `void`
  and swallowed every throw, `recordMachinePaymentEvidenceBaseById` no-opped on
  an unreadable intent, and `recordMachinePaymentEvidenceBase` returned early
  and silently on four conditions including a missing `resource_url`. It then
  incremented `confirmed` and logged "Settlement sweep completed an unreported
  erc7710 payment" either way. Because the confirm has already flipped the
  intent `submitted → confirmed`, the payment permanently left
  `FIND_SWEEPABLE_ERC7710_INTENTS_SQL`, `observeErc7710Settlement` answered
  `not_applicable` on an already-confirmed intent, and the reporting backfill
  selects from evidence ROWS — so the payment ended settled, absent from the
  user's books, unreached by anything automated, and reported as a success. (An
  agent re-posting the same hash would still have completed it: the attach
  handler falls through to the same evidence write. The gap was that nothing
  prompts a second report, and the tick's success log removed the only signal
  that one was needed.) That is #2117's
  own failure mode, re-created by #2117's fix and silenced. Found by the #2136
  comparison against PR #2134, the parallel never-merged implementation, whose
  author (PhilipEriksson) caught it in his own reviewer pass and fixed it there
  first; the observability half of this change is his design.
  **Ordering was examined and cannot be the remedy.** The evidence write
  structurally requires the confirm — `recordMachinePaymentEvidenceBase` refuses
  any intent not already `confirmed` with a `tx_hash`, and reads `confirmed_at`
  off the row — so evidence is settlement-time proof by construction and the two
  writes cannot be reordered. They cannot share a transaction either: the
  confirm is a guarded CAS serialized per hash by `pg_advisory_xact_lock`, while
  the evidence write is a separate upsert plus a fee-ledger row plus a
  fire-and-forget call to the reporting feed that no rollback could retract. And
  the confirm should not be undone in any case: it records a true fact about the
  chain, and reverting it would make the database assert something false and
  re-open the replay/ambiguity surface the CAS exists to close. This is stated
  explicitly because a fix that only logged the failure would still have left
  the payment stranded.
  **What the fallback guarantees instead.** A recovery pass runs on every tick
  over a new query — confirmed erc7710 intents with no evidence row, inside the
  same 24-hour horizon — and writes the missing row. It re-derives the hole from
  state rather than from a remembered failure, so it also recovers a hole dug by
  the agent-reported path; it touches no chain, because those payments are
  already attributed; and its write is the same idempotent upsert, so racing
  anything else costs nothing. The guarantee is therefore not "the failure
  cannot happen" but "the failure leaves the payment inside a retry path rather
  than outside every one", which is the property that was missing.
  **Nothing to record vs failed to record.** The seam's early returns are now
  classified rather than uniformly silent. `not_protocol_rail` and `not_settled`
  are **nothing to record** — correct answers to a speculative caller such as
  `POST /payments`, which reaches the seam on rows that may not be
  evidence-bearing at all. `missing_resource_url`, `intent_not_found` and
  `write_threw` are **failures**: a payment that should be in the books is not.
  `machine_payment_evidence.resource_url` is NOT NULL, so a settled x402 intent
  without one is unwritable rather than merely unwritten; it is retried until the
  horizon and warned on every attempt. At the sweep's own call site there is no
  legitimate "nothing to record" left, since it has just established every
  precondition itself, so it treats every non-`recorded` outcome as a failure.
  **Admission control is narrower, never wider.** The recovery query requires
  `status = 'confirmed'` — a `submitted` intent that already carries a hash (the
  eip3009 in-flight state) is excluded, so no evidence row can be booked for a
  settlement Haven has not verified. Every conjunct is exercised on both sides on
  a real Postgres schema and registered in the `db:schema-smoke` curated
  money-path list. The forward sweep's verification path — checks 1–8,
  `requireDelegationBound`, the replay guard and the ambiguity guard — is
  untouched.
  **Twelve mutations, twelve kills**, each landing confirmed against git before
  its result was read and each restore verified clean against git, with positive
  controls on the seam and on both queries so "records nothing, ever" and
  "returns nothing, ever" cannot pass. Two mutations initially SURVIVED and each
  exposed a real hole that was then closed: widening the recovery query's status
  predicate survived because no seeded row was `submitted` *with* a hash (the
  eip3009 in-flight state), and removing the recovery pass's backoff survived
  because nothing asserted that an unwritable payment is retried on a schedule
  rather than on every tick for a day. The second was found by the independent
  reviewer, not by the author.
