  **The two corrections, and why they are not cosmetic.** `settlementScheme`
  and `facilitatorAddresses` are sent by the published SDK and read by the
  handler, and neither was declared on a closed `X402AuthorizeRequest`.
  Enforcing without them would refuse every erc7710 payment; a compiler with
  ajv's default `removeAdditional: true` — which this plugin deliberately does
  not set — would instead STRIP the facilitator pin and let the payment
  reroute to the 3009 bridge unpinned. Both are declared now (an enum; a 1–16
  address array), both are loosenings of a closed schema, and a test proves an
  undeclared field is refused rather than stripped.
  **What moved and what did not.** The handler's shape rungs are deleted —
  required-ness, string-ness, the address pattern, the digits-only amount, the
  `idempotencyKey` bound, the scheme enum, the facilitator array, the
  `mcpCallContext` object, the settle signature pattern — because the schema
  states each one; the refusal code stays 400 and the body becomes the
  plugin's envelope, which names a FIELD and never a rail. What stays in the
  handler is what JSON Schema cannot say: a non-zero amount, a network this
  agent's chain can settle, and the 64 KB bound on the free-form
  `paymentRequired` blob.
  **One floor had to be restored** (independent review, S1). With coercion on,
  `maxTimeoutSeconds: null | false | Infinity` all arrive as `0`, and `0`
  SURVIVES the `?? 300` default in `modules/x402/delegation-authorize.ts`, so
  the settlement child would silently expire in 60 s — the clamp floor in
  `x402-delegation.ts` — instead of 300, on the route whose whole point is a
  child the merchant must redeem in time; alongside `paymentRequired` it would
  also trip #3117's option match with a message blaming the merchant's
  challenge for the caller's `null`. `minimum: 1` is therefore not a
  narrowing, it IS the deleted rung, and a test pins it. Residual, stated: a
  JSON `true` coerces to `1`, which the floor accepts and the clamp lifts to
  60 s.
  **Enforcement also WIDENS what is accepted, on three inputs.** The plugin
  compiles with ajv `coerceTypes: 'array'` (deliberate and documented at
  `openapi/request-validation.ts`), so where the deleted rungs refused a wrong
  TYPE outright, the schema now converts it: `maxTimeoutSeconds: "300"` is
  accepted and becomes `300`; `amount: 100` is accepted and becomes `"100"`;
  and a bare `facilitatorAddresses: "0x…"` string is accepted and becomes the
  one-element array `["0x…"]`. Each was a 400 at the base. The values are the
  caller's own — nothing is invented and nothing is dropped — but this is a
  widening on a money route and it is recorded here rather than left as an
  equivalence. Measured on the compiled `X402AuthorizeRequest` with the
  plugin's real options; the 24 h reading logged zero `would_coerce`, so no
  dev traffic exercised it.
  `paymentRequired` stays `additionalProperties: true`
  ON PURPOSE (#1355): it is the merchant's own parsed 402 challenge, verified
  at the signer against the Haven-signed expected context, never trusted as
  authority here — declaring its inner shape would make every merchant field
  variation a refusal on a money route, which no reading supports. The #2245
  property holds: a retired-rail account's 410 is still the answer to a
  conformant request, and the structural refusal that precedes it still makes
  no rail claim.
  **One ordering change, stated.** A malformed request on a retired-rail
  account now meets the schema's 400 before the rail's 410 — five test
  fixtures carried a placeholder signature (`0xsig`) that the declared
  `^0x[0-9a-fA-F]{130}$` refuses. The fixtures are made conformant and the
  ordering is pinned by its own test rather than left to surface as a rail
  regression.
