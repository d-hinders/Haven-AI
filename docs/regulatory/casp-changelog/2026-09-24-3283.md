- **#3283** (epic #3284) — `@haven_ai/sdk`'s in-process signing gets the same signing-surface guard as the edge signer. **The perimeter narrows.** Before this change, `HavenClient.signForData` signed whatever the Haven API response handed it, after only the #3271 binding check. That check proves the typed data and its hash describe the same operation, but both values come from the same response. So a compromised Haven API, or a man-in-the-middle on the agent's HTTPS, was enough to get an SDK-embedded agent (including the local `@haven_ai/mcp` server) to sign a payload that captures the delegate smart account: an empty-context redemption carrying a self-call, or a ROOT-authority settlement child. Three changes:
  - **One guard, in the SDK.** The direct-payment allowlist (`packages/sdk/src/direct-payment-guard.ts`) moved out of `@haven_ai/signer` into `@haven_ai/sdk`, together with the redemption guard, the delegate-account derivation and the settlement-child verifier. The signer imports them, and its refusal (`TYPED_DATA_NOT_ALLOWED`), messages and tool surface are unchanged. `signForData` now signs an `eip712_userop` payload only when it passes the binding **and** the allowlist: its own derived account, a pinned chain, a single `execute` to the DelegationManager calling `redeemDelegations` with exactly one delegation granted to that account by a different account, `SingleDefault` mode, canonical encoding.
  - **Settlement children are verified against the merchant, not against Haven.** On the erc7710 path the SDK checks the child against the merchant's own 402 option: payee, exact amount, token, chain, the advertised facilitators (the redeemer caveat), and an expiry of at most 600 seconds. Haven does not produce that option, so it cannot restate the expectation to match a child it rewrote. `signForData` refuses a child it has no such expectation for.
  - **No root grants, no foreign delegators.** The shared settlement-child verifier now refuses a child whose `authority` is ROOT and, when the caller names its own account, a child delegated by any other account. A root child would have granted its redeemer authority over the agent's own account instead of spending through the budget delegation's enforcers. A non-root authority must resolve on-chain to a delegation made to this account, which only the account owner can sign.

  **Tests.** Each case is pinned by a named test: `packages/sdk/src/signing-guard.test.ts` (the empty-context redemption through `pay()`, a self-call, another account, an unpinned chain) and `sign-scheme-dispatch.test.ts` / `x402-erc7710.test.ts` (a ROOT, caveat-free child; a rewritten payee or amount; unadvertised facilitators; no expectation). Each new guard was removed in turn and its test went red.

  **No custody or execution boundary moves.** Enforcement is still on-chain, and the caveat enforcers bound every redemption. What narrows is what an SDK-embedded delegate key will sign. `HavenClient.sign(hash)` and the exported signing primitives stay verbatim, for embedders. **Residual exposure:**
  - SDK and `@haven_ai/mcp` installs keep the old behaviour until upgraded.
  - Epic #3284 records the accepted residuals: payments within the budget caveats (any recipient, for an open budget), and delegate-EOA balances.

  Perimeter narrowed.
