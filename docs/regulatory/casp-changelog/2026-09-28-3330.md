- **#3330 (epic #3328, slice 2)** — sub-agent budgets: agent A re-delegates a narrower budget to agent B in the same account as an ERC-7710 child of A's budget delegation (`packages/backend/src/modules/sub-budgets/`, `routes/agent-sub-budgets.ts` owner-auth issue/sign/list/tree/revoke, `routes/sub-budgets.ts` agent-auth sign-context lifecycle, migration `100_agent_sub_budgets`, `rails/delegation-authorization.ts` redeeming the three-link `[grant, parent-child, budget]` chain, `modules/x402/x402-delegation.ts` building the settlement child under the grant, `sub_budget_id` on `POST /payments` and `subBudgetId` on `POST /x402/authorize`; `@haven_ai/sdk` `sub-budget-guards.ts` + a three-link case in `redemption-guard.ts`; `@haven_ai/signer` `haven_sign` with `sub_budget_id`; `parent_sub_budgets` additive on `GET /machine-payments/agent`; the parent→child tree on the dashboard agent page).

  Authority and custody argument: the first cross-agent delegation in the rail, and the narrowest possible shape for it. B's grant is a child of A's SELF-delegated parent-child, which is a child of A's owner-granted budget — so every sat B can spend is carved out of what A's own account could already redeem under A's budget, and the budget's period enforcer still meters the whole tree: B's payments revert beyond B's slice AND beyond the parent's remaining period budget, proven by the chain-enforcement test in `sub-budget-delegation.test.ts` (the redemption payload carries the parent's period meter, which still binds). A grant can only narrow — period amount ≤ the parent's on the same window, expiry ≤ the parent's, a recipient pin never unpinned — and the API refuses a wider child in amount, expiry or recipient BEFORE anything is signed (`sub_budget_wider_than_parent`). Haven still signs nothing, holds no key, moves no funds: the owner issues (owner-governed, per the decision log 2026-09-27 — owner co-signs each sub-budget, the safer of the issue's two options, so no agent ever grants standing re-delegation authority), and A's delegate key only signs the already-built children within that owner-approved envelope after the SDK/signer guards re-derive their meaning from the caveats. A's key never produces authority for an account it does not delegate from — the grant's `to` IS B's delegate account, so only B's delegate can redeem it, with no redeemer caveat needed. Revocation cascades the fail-closed direction: the owner revoking A's budget delegation strands every child under it on-chain; A's parent-child revoke strands B's grants; revoking B's grant leaves A's own chain intact (real-DB test `criterion 3`). The redemption allowlist in the SDK widens by one precisely described chain (a leaf delegated BY another agent's account under a self-delegated middle link) and refuses every other shape; the signer's `haven_sign` gains the sub-budget child as a flow-keyed signable verified against the fetched expectation; that widening is stated in `delegation-rail-security-model.md` §10 (amended, not loosened, with a dated re-verified note).
