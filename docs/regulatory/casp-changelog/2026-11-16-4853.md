- **#4853** — the demo merchant's joke product is paid to a Stripe deposit
  address and each settled payment is recorded in Stripe as a PaymentIntent in
  `transaction_verification` mode. The merchant still sells only its own demo
  product; no third-party funds are received or moved, and Haven is not a party
  to the transfer.

  What changed (`packages/demo-merchant-mcp/src/`):
  - **`stripe.ts` (new).** Resolves the two Stripe values (`STRIPE_DEPOSIT_ADDRESS`,
    `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`) and is off — with a warning, never a refusal to start —
    when only one is set, when CDP is not configured, when the recorded
    product's price is below one cent or not a whole number of cents, or when
    the deposit address is not a 20-byte hex address. With neither value set it
    says nothing. `createStripeRecording` creates the PaymentIntent (amount in
    US cents, `currency=usd`, `confirm=true`, `payment_method_types=crypto`,
    `payment_method_options[crypto][mode]=transaction_verification` with
    `network=base` and the transaction hash) with the transaction hash as the
    idempotency key and in `metadata.transaction_hash`. "Already recorded" is a
    read of Stripe (a PaymentIntent search on that metadata), not the
    idempotency key, because Stripe prunes keys after about 24 hours. A failure
    is logged as `[stripe] record_failed tx=<hash> reason=<type/code>` and
    counted; the key is never logged, and `record()` never rejects.
  - **`products.ts`.** The joke carries `stripeRecording: true`, and
    `productReceivingAddress` returns the deposit address for that product only
    when Stripe is on. No other product can name it.
  - **`http.ts`.** The 402's `payTo`, the verification and the invoice's
    recipient address follow the product's receiving address, so the joke's
    quote names the deposit address. After settlement the payment is recorded
    fire-and-forget — the money has already moved, so a Stripe refusal, timeout
    or network error cannot delay or refuse the goods. `/healthz` gains a
    `stripe` block with `recorded` and `record_failures`, and no credential.
  - **`x402.ts`.** Review S7: a redeploy that changes the deposit address no
    longer refuses a buyer whose authorization already settled to the address it
    was quoted for. The quote checks still run first; on their failure the chain
    is read, and a used authorization whose transaction paid an address the
    merchant quoted before — named by the operator in
    `STRIPE_PREVIOUS_DEPOSIT_ADDRESS`, never taken from the payment — the
    product's price is served with that hash. An unused one, or one addressed to
    an address the merchant never quoted, keeps the original refusal. The
    operator-supplied address is the point: the payment's own `payTo` is
    attacker-chosen, so honouring it would hand goods to anyone who paid an
    address of their own.
  - **`stripe-reconcile.ts` / `reconcile-cli.ts` (new).** The reconciliation
    script lists the settled payments to the deposit address that have no
    PaymentIntent and records them, over a block range the operator passes.
    Only an EIP-3009 transfer of the product's exact price is recorded; any
    other transfer is listed as `not_recorded` and left alone, because recording
    a transfer CDP did not settle needs Stripe's confirmation first (epic #4849,
    Out of scope). Idempotent: a second run creates nothing.

  Why the perimeter is unaffected:
  - The merchant remains the seller of its own demo product. The deposit address
    is the merchant's own Stripe account's address; the USDC moves from the
    payer straight to it, and Haven neither receives, holds nor settles it.
  - The Stripe key can create and read PaymentIntents and nothing else. A
    PaymentIntent in `transaction_verification` mode records a transfer the
    payer already signed and CDP already settled; it cannot move funds, change
    the price, alter the signed payment intent or gate a purchase.
  - Recording is downstream of settlement and cannot refuse a buyer: a Stripe
    failure is logged and counted, and the goods are delivered. The one payment
    path change (S7) only widens what is served — a buyer whose money moved is
    never refused — and never what is settled.
  - No key, route, scope, authority surface or settlement step changes. The
    reconciliation script runs inside the merchant service's environment with
    that service's variables; its report carries transaction hashes and
    PaymentIntent IDs only.
