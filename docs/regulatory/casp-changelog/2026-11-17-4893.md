- **#4893 (first PR)**: the merchant kit's paywall proxy,
  `@haven_ai/paywall-proxy` (`packages/paywall-proxy/`, workspace-private). It
  puts x402 in front of any HTTP API on behalf of a merchant. It is built to
  `docs/architecture/12-merchant-kit.md` and settles through the shared core
  `@haven_ai/merchant-payments` (#4890). The merchant receives every payment
  directly at its own `PAY_TO` address. Haven is not in the flow: it does not
  operate the proxy, receives nothing, and holds no key the proxy uses.

  What it does:
  - Answers an unpaid request for a priced route with an x402 challenge.
    The price comes from the products file for that route and product, never
    from the payment. The network is one setting, and the proxy refuses to start
    without its receiving address, network, operator name, products file, store,
    upstream, CDP credentials or RPC.
  - Before any facilitator call, refuses a request whose product, model,
    output-token cap or body does not fit, and a payment that does not match
    the challenge, including one that names no resource. What binds a fresh
    payment to its product is the amount and payee it signed. The resource is
    not signed, so a buyer can rewrite it, and it adds no protection between
    two products of the same price; a known payment is bound by the product
    recorded for it.
  - Verifies and settles through the core's Coinbase path
    (`verifyCdpAuthorization`, `settleThroughCdp`, `recoverUsedAuthorization`).
    The proxy has **no self-settlement path and no settlement key, and never
    submits a transaction** (a source-scan test asserts it).
  - Keeps the consumed-payment store of §6 in `node:sqlite`:
    - The store file is held under an exclusive lock, so a second process
      refuses to start.
    - The reservation token never leaves memory and is cleared at startup.
    - Every transition is one conditional update.
    - A delivered payment never buys a second call, including after a restart.
    - The one forward that needs no proof is a settlement this proxy itself
      sent. A transfer found on the chain that it never sent may be anyone's
      replay of the public authorization (another proxy on the same `PAY_TO`,
      a store restored from a backup), so it is recorded `settled` and needs a
      proof. The design's §6 is amended to match (found in review).
  - Forwards only after settlement is confirmed:
    - The buyer's payment, proof, tax-declaration and authorization headers are
      removed.
    - The merchant's upstream key is added, replacing any buyer value. The
      `Expect` header and any header the buyer names in `Connection` are
      removed too (Node's `fetch` refuses `Expect`, which would have failed
      every settled call; found in review).
    - The output-token cap is set or lowered, never raised.
    - Answers carry `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` with the settlement transaction.
    - An upstream failure after settlement is answered 200 with the
      transaction hash.
  - The upstream key and the CDP secret appear in no response and no log line.
    Tests capture both, including a facilitator error that echoes the secret.

  Why the perimeter is unaffected:
  - Haven receives, holds and moves nothing here. The merchant runs the proxy,
    the buyer pays the merchant's address directly through Coinbase's
    facilitator, and the proxy holds no key that can sign a transfer (its
    Coinbase API secret only authenticates API requests).
  - Haven's own buyer-side paths and custody posture do not change. The core's
    settlement code is reused unchanged; the core gains only
    `authorizationDigest`, the §6 digest.
  - Money-path list: `packages/paywall-proxy/src/**` is a control glob, by the
    owner decision on #4893 (2026-11-16). It moves to the runtime list when
    #4892 deploys the proxy with a harness address.

  Not in this PR (the second #4893 PR):
  - proof-backed (`SIGN-IN-WITH-X`) retries of a failed or interrupted call,
    and the challenge nonces they use;
  - per-IP rate limits, the verify cap, the upstream slot cap and the
    `at_capacity` answers.

  Until that PR lands, a re-presented settled payment is answered
  `payment_retry_needs_proof` and is never forwarded. The discovery document
  (#4891), receipts (#4913) and sales reporting (#4898) are separate issues.

  Tests: the proxy's suite runs against a mocked facilitator, chain reader and
  upstream, with a buyer that signs real EIP-3009 authorizations. It has 113
  tests, mapped criterion by criterion in the PR. The core's
  `authorizationDigest` (the §6 digest) has its own tests. Mutation cells,
  each going red: the missing-resource refusal disabled (the same-price
  cross-product test); a chain-found transfer forwarded as the holder's own
  (the replay test); `Expect` forwarded (the real-fetch test). The same PR
  bounds `UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT_MS` and `RESERVATION_DEADLINE_MS` to the design's
  values, bounds the lost-settle poll by its deadline, and releases a
  reservation only when the settle call was provably never sent.

  Perimeter unchanged.
